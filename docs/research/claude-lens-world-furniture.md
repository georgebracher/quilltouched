# Isekai World Furniture — Lore Bible Checklist for Everquill (Team B)

**Framing note before the checklist.** Isekai furniture is *game-mechanics-made-diegetic*: status screens, ranks, skill grants, drop tables. Everquill's core architecture (hidden TRUE sheets, per-character KNOWN sheets, intel that can be confidently wrong) is a near-perfect host for this furniture with one twist that should be the lore bible's spine: **in Everquill, every "game-like" institution is a social/epistemic technology, not an oracle.** Guild ranks, appraisal ceremonies, monster tier charts, potion grades — all of them are *the world's own attempt to build KNOWN sheets*, and all of them can be wrong. Players get the shape they expect from anime; the engine keeps the truth. That single move converts every trope below from set dressing into gameplay.

---

## 1. Adventurer Guild — registration scene

- **Expected form:** A guild hall in every town: reception counter (usually a pretty/long-suffering receptionist — a stock character in her own right), rowdy common room, material-buying counter in back. Registration is same-day and cheap: fill a form (or can't write — receptionist helps), pay a small fee, receive a **guild card**. Card is frequently blood-bound or mana-bound at issue — a drop of blood on the card or a hand on a crystal — making it ID, rank badge, and sometimes a debit card in one ([Tondemo Skill guild](https://tondemo-skill.fandom.com/wiki/Adventurers_Guild), [Bookworm guild card](https://ascendance-of-a-bookworm.fandom.com/wiki/Guild_Card), [Smartphone guild](https://isekai-smartphone.fandom.com/wiki/Adventurer_Guild)). Obligatory beat: a thug sneers at the newbie, gets humiliated.
- **Why expected:** It's the isekai "character creation screen" — the ritual that turns an outsider into a playable person. Nearly every guild-era isekai opens here; the trope descends from JRPG quest hubs ([Frank Mitchell on the guild's origins](https://www.frank-mitchell.com/posts/adventurers-guild-revisited/)).
- **Everquill fit:** Make registration a real early scene with mechanical teeth. The blood-drop card is the world's KNOWN-sheet artifact: the guild records what it *observed at intake* (a test cut, a lift, a Q&A), producing a public, partial, possibly-wrong sheet. Card doubles as the diegetic anchor for the deed log — deeds reported to the guild propagate as rumor faster and more accurately than deeds nobody filed. The "thug tests the newbie" beat is a free tutorial for the plausibility ladder (punching the veteran is *hopeless*, not impossible). Low-magic note: card can be wax-seal + guild cipher rather than mana-tech; a *single* sealed law-artifact card at HQ is a good way to keep it special.

## 2. Quest boards, ranks, and guild bureaucracy

- **Expected form:** Corkboard of paper requests sorted by rank; ranks run **F/G up through A, then S** (SS for legends). Rank gates quest access "so rookies don't die"; completing quests earns points, abandoning them loses points; herb-gathering and horned-rabbit culls are the canonical F-rank jobs ([guild trope roundup](https://www.scribblehubforum.com/threads/the-concept-of-adventurers-guild.8481/), [Tondemo Skill](https://tondemo-skill.fandom.com/wiki/Adventurers_Guild)). Boards also carry standing bounties, escort jobs, and emergency all-hands calls (stampede/horde events).
- **Why expected:** The board is the isekai quest-log UI. F-to-S is so standardized that audiences read "B-rank" as a precise power statement without explanation.
- **Everquill fit:** Keep F-to-S literally — it's free legibility on mobile and slots into the Codex as fact cards ("Ironvale Guild rates the Mirefang pack: B"). Crucial Everquill twist: **rank is the guild's KNOWN data, not TRUE data.** A "C-rank" monster the guild has only seen twice may be TRUE-lethal; a feared "A-rank" bandit may be a bluffer. Failed guild intelligence = confidently-wrong fact cards players can disprove through research — exactly your intel loop. Quest board is also your world-time surface: postings age, expire, get sniped by NPC parties (deed log for NPCs), and new postings arrive on real-time cadence — a natural, fair-feeling gate. Player rank should rise off the deed log (witnessed deeds), so reputation and rank are the same system.

## 3. Taverns and inns

- **Expected form:** Attached to or next to the guild. Ale, hard bread, mystery-meat stew (usually disappointing — feeds trope #14), a barmaid, a jobs-gossip ecosystem. Inn pricing quoted in coppers per night, meals included or not; "room with bath" is a luxury flag. The tavern is where rumors, party recruitment, and info brokers live.
- **Why expected:** The tavern is the social hub screen; anime uses it as the exposition room between arcs.
- **Everquill fit:** This is your **rumor-propagation reification**. Tavern = the place where the deed log becomes audible: overhear distorted versions of your own deeds (and your dead predecessor's), buy drinks to raise rumor fidelity, catch confidently-wrong intel from drunks (world-generated false fact cards). Inn sleep is a clean world-time sink (rest quality affecting recovery justifies price tiers). One tappable tavern sprite tableau per town with persistent regulars gives huge continuity value for cheap.

## 4. Dungeons and dungeon economics

- **Expected form:** Discrete, dive-able underground structures; floors get harder with depth; monsters drop **magic stones/cores** harvested from corpses; boss floors; sometimes a **dungeon core** whose destruction kills the dungeon. Whole cities exist as dungeon boomtowns — the dungeon is the local export industry (stones = fuel/currency), with the guild taxing and buying drops ([Dungeon-Based Economy](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/DungeonBasedEconomy), [DanMachi](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Literature/IsItWrongToTryToPickUpGirlsInADungeon)). Common law: dungeons "respawn" monsters, making them renewable resources.
- **Why expected:** DanMachi, Solo Leveling-adjacent works, and dozens of dungeon-core novels made "dungeon as economic engine" default physics.
- **Everquill fit:** Strong fit *if rationed*. Low-magic Everquill should have **few, named, ancient dungeons** — each a sealed-tier mystery ("why does the Undercroft restock?") rather than ambient infrastructure. Dungeon economics is a gift to your systems: floor maps are Codex map content; depth is a legible danger ladder (floor 3 routine → floor 9 hopeless *until intel*); drop markets give currency sinks/sources; a dungeon boomtown is a generated-city archetype that consumes player history well ("the party that cleared floor 6 drinks free here"). The *dungeon core* should be a component-law feat (sealed tier), not a farmable object. Respawn, if kept, is a researchable law — one of the best hypothesis-pinboard questions you can hand players.

## 5. Demon lord / hero summoning geopolitics

- **Expected form:** A human-race alliance church/kingdom bloc vs a **Demon Lord's territory** of mazoku; periodic wars; and the **hero summoning ritual** — kingdoms yank otherworlders in as weapons, with the dark modern reading that summoning is exploitative: heroes as state assets, coerced, discarded, or soul-bound ([Summon Everyman Hero](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/SummonEverymanHero); Slime's abused summoning rites; [Realist Hero](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Literature/HowARealistHeroRebuiltTheKingdom) summoned as a *war-tribute bargaining chip*). Standard race-politics furniture: humans dominant and prejudiced, elves withdrawn, dwarves allied craftsmen, beastfolk discriminated against, demons "dangerous but not universally evil" ([Standard Japanese Fantasy Setting](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/StandardJapaneseFantasySetting), [Fantastic Racism](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/FantasticRacism)).
- **Why expected:** It's the genre's macro-plot default; post-2015 audiences specifically expect the *cynical* version (church/kingdom is corrupt, demon lord is misunderstood or a fabricated threat).
- **Everquill fit:** Perfect sealed-tier material. Make hero summoning a **historical component-law feat** — done once or twice centuries ago, components lost/controlled, consequences still scarring the map. "Is the Demon Lord real, dead, a committee, or church propaganda?" is a world-class hypothesis-pinboard question, and the TRUE/KNOWN split means the church's answer and the truth can differ. Players will *expect to be the summoned hero*; in Everquill they should be locals living in the aftermath of summoned heroes — deed logs of dead legendary "heroes" are great world texture and set the no-plot-armor tone. Geopolitics gives generated cities factional flavor (church city, frontier city, demon-border garrison) cheaply.

## 6. Magic academies

- **Expected form:** Prestigious academy in the capital; entrance exam arc; noble students sneering at commoner prodigies; public duels/arena exhibitions; house/class rankings; a token kind teacher ([Wizarding School](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/WizardingSchool); the noble-vs-commoner duel formula is standardized enough to be [parodied wholesale](https://www.fanfiction.net/s/14255045/1/Average-Isekai-Magic-Academy-be-like)).
- **Why expected:** Academy arcs are the genre's second-most-common opening after guild registration.
- **Everquill fit:** In low-magic Everquill, reframe as **the Academy/College of a scarce art** — a university of law-study (alchemy, chirurgery, the sealed arts) rather than a fireball school. It becomes the *institutional research vendor*: paying tuition or trading intel buys access to archives (fact cards), tutors (turn wrong intel right), and laboratories (component identification for sealed feats). Entrance exam + noble gatekeeping maps directly onto nobles-vs-commoners friction (#12) and gives a non-combat progression track. Academy library = the premium world-time sink (research takes days; players will feel that's *right* here).

## 7. Appraisal ceremony / skill church / status screens

- **Expected form:** Two fused tropes. (a) **Status screen:** say "Status" and a floating window lists level/skills/titles ([geeknabe on game-like skills](https://geeknabe.com/blog/4-game-like-skills-you-often-see-in-isekai-manga/)). (b) **Church ceremony:** at a set age (5, 7, 10, 12 by series), children are taken to the temple, touch a crystal/statue, and a priest reads out their god-given blessing/skill/talent grade — publicly, socially binding, with "trash skill" kids disowned and prodigies recruited ([Bookworm baptism](https://ascendance-of-a-bookworm.fandom.com/wiki/Baptism), [Aristocrat Reborn's 7-gods leveled blessings](https://chronicles-of-an-aristocrat.fandom.com/wiki/Seven_Pillars), [Appraiser LN](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Literature/TheStrongestJobIsApparentlyNotAHeroOrASageButAnAppraiser)). Appraisal-as-skill (inspect anything, see its stats) is its own beloved subgenre.
- **Why expected:** It's the genre's literalized character sheet; the "trash-skill kid vindicated" plot is one of isekai's top-three fantasies.
- **Everquill fit:** **This is your flagship adaptation — do not import literally.** Everquill *has no readable TRUE sheet*, which sounds like breaking the trope but is actually the strongest version of it: make the **Skill Church a real institution whose appraisals are fallible KNOWN-sheet entries.** The ceremony happens, the priest pronounces "Talentless" or "Sword-Blessed," it goes on your public record and shapes NPC reactions — but it's the church's *guess*, sometimes politically bent, sometimes flat wrong. "The church misjudged me" is the trash-skill fantasy with epistemic teeth, and a wrong appraisal on the player's own heir is an inheritance-arc generator. Appraisal-the-action = your research verb wearing genre clothes: an appraiser NPC or learned technique that writes intel to the Codex, with failure writing confident nonsense. Never show a floating status window in narration; show *documents, plaques, priests, and cards* that carry known-only data.

## 8. Monster taxonomy — slimes to dragons

- **Expected form:** A shared bestiary ladder: **slime → goblin → wolf/horned rabbit → orc → ogre → wyvern → dragon**, with undead, golems, and demons as parallel tracks; guilds label monsters with the same F–S/A–B letter grades as adventurers; calamity-class beings (true dragons, demon lords) rate "no nation can solo this" ([Slime's rank system](https://tensura.fandom.com/wiki/Rank), [common isekai monsters](https://www.sportskeeda.com/anime/10-common-isekai-anime-monsters)). Slimes are the joke-tier tutorial kill (with the wink that *sometimes* a slime is secretly apex); goblins are the first real fight and often the first moral queasiness; dragons are the yardstick of all power.
- **Why expected:** Direct Dragon Quest inheritance; the ladder is the audience's pre-installed danger UI.
- **Everquill fit:** Adopt the ladder's *names and silhouettes* wholesale — it's free legibility for the sprite tableau and plausibility ladder (slime = trivial, goblin band = routine, ogre = desperate, wyvern = hopeless, dragon = sealed/impossible territory). Then run your signature move on it: the guild's letter grade is KNOWN, the TRUE sheet varies per individual (an old scarred goblin chief can out-stat a young ogre), and **intel moves fights down the ladder** — learning the wyvern's molt-week turns hopeless into desperate, which is your core loop stated in monster terms. Keep dragon count near-single-digits and named; in low magic, one confirmed living dragon should warp regional politics. The "slime that isn't joke-tier" is a fine one-off rumor payload.

## 9. Currency — copper/silver/gold

- **Expected form:** **Gold–silver–copper decimal standard**: 1 gold = 10 silver = 100 copper is the modal rate, with big-purse denominations (large gold, platinum, white gold) above and iron/small copper below. Genre-wide mental anchor: **1 gold ≈ ¥100,000**, copper ≈ small change; a night at an inn is a few coppers to a silver, a commoner family lives on a few silver a month ([Gold–Silver–Copper Standard](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/GoldSilverCopperStandard), [gold coin isekai conventions](https://grokipedia.com/page/Gold_coin_isekai), [Overlord currency](https://overlordmaruyama.fandom.com/wiki/Currency)). Stock scenes: first quest payout counted coin by coin; sticker shock at potion prices; guild card as bank account.
- **Why expected:** Universal across the genre; players arrive knowing what a silver "feels like."
- **Everquill fit:** Adopt without cleverness — exotic currencies burn player attention for no gain. Fix one internal anchor (inn night = 3 copper; F-rank herb job = 2–5 copper; healing draught = 1–2 silver; sword = 1 gold) and let the Codex auto-card local price intel. Two Everquill-native wrinkles worth having: regional coinage trust (frontier towns discount capital-minted coin — travel/trade texture) and coin as rumor vector (spending big in a tavern *is* publishing your wealth to the deed/rumor system).

## 10. Potions

- **Expected form:** Red healing potions in glass vials, graded (low/mid/high/full or E–S), drunk or splashed; mana potions blue; antidotes green; elixirs as near-mythic full-restores. Sold at guild counters and apothecaries; expensive enough that rookies ration them; potion-making is a viable protagonist career ([Healing Potion trope](https://allthetropes.org/wiki/Healing_Potion), [I Shall Survive Using Potions!](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Literature/IShallSurviveUsingPotions)).
- **Why expected:** JRPG item-slot furniture; the vial silhouette alone reads instantly on mobile.
- **Everquill fit:** Keep potions but pull them down the magic ladder: **apothecary craft, not miracle water** — a "healing draught" stops bleeding, dulls pain, fights infection over days of world-time (which monetizes beautifully with time-skips) rather than re-growing limbs. Grades = the *seller's claim* (KNOWN); adulterated or mislabeled potions are natural bad-intel events, and appraising a potion before drinking is a real research micro-action. True instant-restore elixirs belong at sealed tier with component laws — a perfect long-arc research goal. Pack screen benefits from the classic color-coding regardless.

## 11. Carriages and travel

- **Expected form:** Distance measured in **days by carriage**; scheduled merchant caravans between towns; escort quests as the rookie's first real job; highway bandits and monster attacks as en-route content; toll gates and city walls with entry checks (guild card waves you through); horse-drawn cart for commoners, crested coach for nobles, dragon/griffin for the exotic ([I Shall Survive Using Potions! travel structure](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Literature/IShallSurviveUsingPotions) is typical of the town-to-town cadence).
- **Why expected:** Travel-days are the genre's map scale; escort-quest-then-bandits is a stock arc.
- **Everquill fit:** **This is your monetization spine wearing genre clothes.** World-time travel gating is *already* how isekai narrates distance, so paid time-skips read as diegetic ("the road was uneventful") instead of gamey. Design travel as choices, not waiting: caravan (slow, safe, social — rumor intake en route) vs solo/fast (cheaper, riskier, rolls on the road) vs paying premium passage. City gate checks are where the deed log bites — guards have heard of you, accurately or not. Escort jobs bridge combat and economy and introduce recurring merchant NPCs (great rumor carriers between cities).

## 12. Nobles vs commoners

- **Expected form:** Rigid class society: nobles hold magic/talent monopolies and legal privilege; the arrogant blond noble (ojou-sama/oresama) who insults the commoner protagonist and eats a public humiliation duel; adventurers as the classless escape valve ("the guild doesn't care about your birth"); corrupt lord as arc villain; royalty granting titles to strong adventurers ([noble reincarnation catalogue](https://www.cbr.com/isekai-manga-protagonists-reincarnated-as-nobles/), academy-discrimination furniture above).
- **Why expected:** It's the genre's default social physics and the engine of its most reliable catharsis (merit beats birth).
- **Everquill fit:** High value, low cost. Nobles are ideal KNOWN-sheet gameplay: a baron's *reputation* (feared duelist, wise steward) is rumor data that may not match his TRUE sheet — social research is investigation gameplay with zero new systems. The guild-as-meritocracy gives classless players a lane while noble society gives the deed log somewhere to matter (deeds convert to invitations, patronage, titles). Heir mechanic hooks in hard: inherited debts, feuds, and a family's standing are exactly "thin inheritance" content. Skip the humiliation-duel *guarantee* — in a no-plot-armor game, insulting a duke should be genuinely dangerous, which is itself a fresh take players will respect.

## 13. Otherworld foods

- **Expected form:** Fantasy-world food is bland (unseasoned, black hard bread, boiled mystery meat); protagonist introduces rice, soy sauce, **mayonnaise** (a full-blown genre in-joke), curry, or just "cooking with care," and locals react with ecstatic disbelief; food becomes wealth, diplomacy, or monster-taming currency ([isekai food trope](https://www.dualshockers.com/best-isekai-anime-tropes/), [Campfire Cooking](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Literature/CampfireCookingInAnotherWorldWithMyAbsurdSkill)).
- **Why expected:** Food-isekai is one of the genre's biggest subgenres; the "mayo moment" is a meme audiences actively await.
- **Everquill fit:** Careful — the trope assumes an *outsider with Earth knowledge*, which Everquill's local-born characters don't have. Adaptation: make cuisine **regional intel**. Recipes, spice routes, and preservation techniques are researchable knowledge that travels with the player (and survives death via journals — a lovely heir beat: grandmother's recipe book). Monster-part cooking ("is wyvern tail edible?") is genuinely fun research-action content with wrong-intel comedy built in (confidently-wrong Codex card: "marsh eel is a delicacy" — it is not). Inn food quality tiers give taverns texture. Don't do the mayo bit; players know it's an outsider joke and it would crack the locked-universe frame.

## 14. Bonus furniture worth stocking (brief)

- **Demihuman races:** elves (long-lived, withdrawn), dwarves (smiths, allied), beastfolk (discriminated frontier folk) — audiences expect the set ([Standard Fantasy Races](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/StandardFantasyRaces)); in low magic, keep them rare/regional and make racial politics rumor-driven rather than stat-driven.
- **Info broker / merchant guild / thieves' guild:** expected parallel institutions; all three are natural intel vendors (and sellers of *deliberately* wrong intel — a legitimate antagonist for an epistemic game).
- **Skill church relics & holy sword in a vault:** expected; both slot into sealed-tier component laws.
- **Stampede/horde event:** periodic monster surge threatening a town; expected dungeon-adjacent set piece; excellent as a scheduled world-time event the whole player-adjacent region hears about via rumor.

## Handle carefully / avoid

- **Slavery (AVOID as content, at most exists as villainy):** Slave-collar companions and "bought the catgirl" beats are heavily criticized as the genre's worst habit — slavery played as romance-acquisition or convenience, never examined ([CBR on Shield Hero's subplot](https://www.cbr.com/shield-hero-season-3-slavery-slaves-worst-isekai-anime-subplot/), [CBR genre analysis](https://www.cbr.com/isekai-anime-worlds-slavery-theme/), [Tales From Square One](https://talesfromsquareone.wordpress.com/2023/09/22/the-problem-with-isekai-2-the-slavery-issue/)). Mobile storefront risk is real on top of the ethical read. If bondage/indenture exists in the world at all, keep it as *antagonist infrastructure the player can only oppose*, never own, and never as a companion-acquisition or romance channel. Debt-bondage as a researched social evil (freeing someone via legal/intel play) is the one defensible use.
- **Harem framing (AVOID):** Collector-romance where every woman orbits the protagonist is the genre's second-most-mocked element and clashes with a no-plot-armor world where NPCs have TRUE sheets and their own deeds. Individual, earned relationships that live in the rumor system ("she was seen with the Ashford heir") are strictly better content. No affection meters.
- **Status-screen literalism (avoid in narration):** Floating windows and numeric levels would violate the KNOWN-only narration contract and cheapen the epistemic game. All "screens" must be diegetic documents (guild card, appraisal certificate, bestiary page) with fallible contents.
- **Summoned-protagonist expectation (manage, don't serve):** Isekai-lean players may expect to *be* the otherworlder. Everquill's answer — you are local, the summonings already happened, their wreckage is the mystery — should be stated early and confidently in the fiction so it reads as premise, not absence.
- **Underage-coded companions / "she's actually 500 years old" (avoid):** Standard genre liability; simply don't stock it.
- **Cheat-skill power fantasy (redirect):** The genre's "I got the OP skill" high must be re-channeled into Everquill's actual fantasy: *I found out something nobody else knows.* Intel is the cheat skill. Say this in the lore bible in exactly those terms so writers aim narration at it.
- **Church = cartoon evil (moderate):** Corrupt-church is now the *expected* twist; a Skill Church that is sincere, useful, and *merely fallible* is both fresher and mechanically richer for the KNOWN-sheet system. Keep corruption local (a bishop, not the faith).
- **Goblin extermination played casually (moderate):** Post-Goblin Slayer audiences accept dark goblin content, but casual genocide-as-chores sits badly with a persistent-world deed log. If every kill is logged and remembered, let the world occasionally remember it *uncomfortably* — cheap moral texture that fits the engine.
