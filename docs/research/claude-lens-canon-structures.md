# Isekai Canon Structures — Research Report for Everquill

## 1. Entry Mechanisms

### Canon taxonomy
Wikipedia's isekai analysis splits transport into six categories: **reincarnation, divine act, summoning, specific tool, pulled-into-a-game, no explanation**; the umbrella split is *tensei* (reincarnation — die, reborn with memories) vs *ten'i* (transition — moved bodily). Truck-kun is the memetic death vector: in a 2017–18 survey of isekai protagonist deaths, "traffic accident by truck" ranked third with 37 cases, and the trope is now so self-aware that series parody it on-screen (Konosuba's tractor "death", Zom 100's truck cameo).

| Mechanism | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Some framed entry event (any type) | **LOAD-BEARING** | An isekai lean without an explicit crossing scene feels wrong. The entry scene does three jobs: licenses the player's out-of-world knowledge, establishes the fish-out-of-water POV, and is the genre's handshake. It's the single scene the audience will screenshot. |
| Summoning (circle, altar, ritual, god) | Load-bearing *variant* | Best fit for session-one character creation: the world pulls you in with a purpose. Genre precedent for **mass/parallel summoning** (Shield Hero's four heroes, mass hero-summon subgenre) covers "why are there many otherworlders" if every player enters this way. |
| Reincarnation with retained memories | Load-bearing *variant* | The other prestige entry (Mushoku Tensei, Slime). Critically: **in-world reincarnation into an heir is genre-native** — dying and continuing as a new person in the same world reads as isekai's own logic, not a game-over screen. |
| Truck-kun specifically | **Optional flavor / parody signal** | It's a meme, and using it sets a comedic, genre-aware tone. Fine as a wink (e.g., a cart, a "runaway wagon-kun" in-joke) but it commits you to self-awareness. Skippable without loss. |
| Transmigration (waking in an existing native's body) | Optional | Popular in webnovel/manhwa space ("villainess" subgenre). Interesting for Everquill heirs: the heir already *has* a life, and the player "arrives" into it — justifies the thin-inheritance seam. |
| VRMMO / trapped-in-a-game | **Avoid** | Canonically a *different* genre (SAO-type). It would frame the System as literal software and invite "log out" questions; clashes with a locked, real universe. |

### Everquill mapping
- **Character creation = the summoning.** Do it diegetically: the ritual/arrival IS the creation flow, with the "who were you before?" questions asked by the summoner or a divine voice. Genre expects the protagonist to be ordinary pre-entry — keep creation light, put weight on what the world gives you.
- **Legacy-on-death = reincarnation/transmigration.** Frame the heir roll explicitly in reincarnation language ("the Quill writes you again"). Journals-carry-knowledge has direct precedent (predecessor's diary/previous-hero's-notes is a stock isekai artifact). Re:Zero proves audiences accept brutal death loops in isekai *when the world's persistence is the point* — your "world stays scarred" is a stronger, fairer version of Return by Death.
- **Conflict:** summoning canonically implies a *summoner with an agenda* (defeat the Demon Lord, then often betrayal — Shield Hero). A hand-authored locked world needs an authored answer to "who summons, and why keep summoning?" This is worldbuilding you must pay for, but it doubles as your excuse for ongoing player influx and for the sealed-feat "laws" (the same laws that permit summoning gate resurrection).

## 2. The "System" — status screens, skills, appraisal, notifications

### Canon expectations
LitRPG/gamified-isekai convention: the world communicates via floating windows ("blue boxes"); the reader/viewer receives the *same* notification the character does; measurable growth is the satisfaction loop; "you know exactly what the protagonist can and can't do, which makes every challenge legible." Status check on arrival — stats, skills, title, blessing — is a stock scene. BUT: a healthy prestige branch of isekai has **no system at all** (Re:Zero, Mushoku Tensei, Ascendance of a Bookworm, Devil is a Part-Timer, Eminence in Shadow), and even within LitRPG there's documented fatigue ("Blue Box Badness") when boxes crowd out prose.

| Element | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| A status/character screen the character can "open" | **LOAD-BEARING for a gamified-isekai *game*** | An anime can skip it; a mobile game leaning isekai cannot — players will expect Status to exist diegetically. But canon does NOT require it to be *complete or truthful* (see appraisal below). |
| Exact numeric stats (STR 47, HP 320) | **Optional — and canonically unreliable** | This is your flagged conflict, and the genre already solved it for you: see below. |
| Appraisal/Identify as an acquired skill | **LOAD-BEARING in system-isekai** | Whole subgenre treats Appraisal as *the* real cheat ("The Strongest Job Is... an Appraiser"; Campfire Cooking's protagonist leans entirely on it). Gathering information as survival is explicitly the fantasy. |
| Appraisal being blocked, tiered, or spoofed | Canon-established | Standard rules: Appraisal has *levels* (low level shows `???`, name-only, or partial data); **Concealment/False Status skills defeat lower-level Appraisal**; appraising something far above you fails or backfires. This is everywhere (Slime's Great Sage limits, Kumo Desu ga, Campfire Cooking's tiered Appraisal). |
| Level-up / skill-gained notification pings | Optional but high-value dopamine | The "[Skill acquired]" toast is beloved; absence is fine (non-system branch) but presence is cheap joy. |
| The System as a character/entity (speaks, judges, is mysterious) | Optional, trending | Solo Leveling / HDI / Omniscient Reader wave made "what IS the System?" a plot engine. Useful if you ever want the Codex itself to have lore. |

### Everquill mapping — this is your strongest alignment
- **Codex/known-sheet = the diegetic System window, and the genre gives you cover for its incompleteness.** Present the known-sheet in full blue-box System dress — brackets, panels, `[Appraisal]` framing — but with canon-legal gaps: `???`, ranges ("Threat: likely Desperate"), and stale entries. The audience already knows the rule "Appraisal shows what your Appraisal level earns you." Your research actions ARE Appraisal leveling, diegetically.
- **Confidently-wrong intel = spoofed appraisal.** Canon precedent (Concealment > Appraisal, False Status skills) means a monster whose Codex card is *wrong* is not a broken UI — it's a monster with Concealment. Players fluent in isekai will read a lying status window as a genre event, not a bug. Consider occasionally *showing* the tell late ("the card was forged" moment).
- **Intel-unlocks = the level-up ping.** You have no XP levels; give the notification dopamine to knowledge instead: `[Codex updated: Marsh Wyrm — weakness recorded]`, `[New action available: Sever the tendon]`. That toast is the level-up scene, and "hopeless → desperate via knowledge" is a legible, screenshottable power-up in exactly the genre's grammar.
- **Status privacy is canon-aligned:** in genre, only you see your own window; the *public* read is a guild appraisal crystal. That maps perfectly to per-character known-sheets — other characters know about you only what the world's instruments (and rumors/deed log) expose.
- **CONFLICT (the big one):** hardcore system-isekai audiences expect *numbers that are true*. You cannot give true numbers without gutting the known/true split. Resolution: never show fake-precise numbers you'd have to walk back on the TRUE sheet; show **precise-looking claims flagged by source** ("per the guild's appraisal", "per Old Marta's account") and let precision itself be earned. Lean on the non-system prestige branch (Re:Zero/Mushoku) for tone cover: Everquill is "system-*flavored*, knowledge-true," not stat-transparent. Do NOT half-ass it by showing true numbers for the player's own sheet only — that teaches players numbers are trustworthy, then betrays them on enemies.

## 3. Guilds and Rank Ladders

### Canon expectations
The Adventurer's Guild is called out repeatedly as the genre's most essential institution: quest board, receptionist, ability appraisal, lodging, party formation, "a starting point and a place to return." Registration is a fixed ritual scene: sign, **drip blood on the card** (binds ownership; card is unusable by others), receive lowest-rank plate. Ranks run F/G→S (letters) or copper→silver→gold→mithril/orichalcum (metals; Overlord, DanMachi-style variants); card/plate color changes on promotion. Stock beats attached: the newbie assigned herb-picking quests; the veteran who tests the rookie; rank-gated quests taken anyway; the "goblin extermination goes wrong" first tragedy; monster-part turn-ins as proof of kill; guild-card-as-passport at city gates.

| Element | Status |
|---|---|
| A guild (or equivalent institution) as hub + quest source | **LOAD-BEARING** — the audience's home base and difficulty legend. Its absence in an isekai-leaning game would be actively felt. |
| Registration scene with card/plate ritual | **LOAD-BEARING** (one-time cost, huge signal). Blood-drop detail is optional but is the scene's signature garnish. |
| Letter/metal rank ladder | **LOAD-BEARING** — F-to-S is the genre's universal difficulty language. |
| Receptionist character (the "guild onee-san") | Optional flavor, cheap and beloved. |
| Monster-part proof-of-kill economy | Optional; pairs nicely with a low-magic material economy. |

### Everquill mapping
- **Rank ladder = public UI for the plausibility ladder.** Quest postings ranked F–S telegraph trivial/routine/desperate/hopeless *diegetically*: an F-plate reading an A-rank posting knows "hopeless" without breaking fiction. The engine's honest difficulty tiers get a canon skin for free. (Keep the mapping fuzzy — rank suggests, TRUE sheet decides — which is itself canon: rank-mismatch disasters are a stock plot.)
- **Guild card = the world-readable slice of the deed log.** The card records registered deeds/kills; rumor propagation covers the *unregistered* ones. Promotion ceremonies = deed-log milestones surfaced. City gate ID checks give generated cities a canonical hook to consume player history ("the card says D-rank, but the Greenhollow story says otherwise…").
- **Guild appraisal service** = diegetic tutorial for the known-sheet: the crystal shows the *guild's* (imperfect, recorded) read of you, teaching the player that all sheets are someone's opinion.
- **World-time monetization fits guild fiction natively:** quests with travel days, materials that take a week to source, guild request queues — real-time gating already sounds like guild bureaucracy, not a paywall.
- **Party registration:** in canon it's a scene (party of N registered jointly, shared quest credit). For a solo mobile game it's **optional** — remap to NPC companion contracts hired through the guild; their KNOWN sheets of *you* (trust, witnessed deeds) make companions the genre's party members without multiplayer.

## 4. Divine Cheats / Blessings at Entry

### Canon expectations
"New Life in Another World Bonus" (TV Tropes): bonuses granted during reincarnation/summoning, either automatic or handed out by the resident god — often in a white-void "god's waiting room" interview where the protagonist negotiates or picks skills. Summoned heroes are *supposed* to receive blessings (played straight so often that "summoned WITHOUT a blessing" is itself a recognized subversion — Chillin' in Another World, Shield Hero's gimped hero, Konosuba's "you may bring one thing" gag where she takes the goddess). Cheat archetypes: Appraisal, item Creation, element/time dominion, absurd mundane tools (Hoe of God), or simply modern-Earth knowledge as the cheat.

| Element | Status |
|---|---|
| The protagonist gets *something* unique at entry | **LOAD-BEARING** — the audience expects the entry scene to hand over a distinguishing gift; arriving with literally nothing reads as a deliberate hard-mode subversion (viable, but it's a statement). |
| That gift being combat-OP | **Optional — and conflicts with you** (see below). |
| God's-waiting-room interview / pick-your-skill scene | Optional, extremely popular; doubles as character creation UI with genre skin. |
| Goddess/god as recurring patron character | Optional flavor. |

### Everquill mapping
- **The cheat IS the Codex.** Canon already validates "information as the cheat" as a top-tier archetype (Appraisal-is-the-real-cheat subgenre). Make the player's blessing the ability to *see System windows at all* — natives have no Codex; the summoned soul does. This (a) delivers the mandatory unique gift, (b) costs zero combat balance in a low-magic no-plot-armor world, (c) makes the whole UI diegetic in one stroke, and (d) explains why heirs keep it: the blessing follows the soul, which *is* your reincarnation loop.
- **God's-waiting-room = character creation screen, played straight.** The interview format natively hosts your creation questions and can seed the first known-sheet entries as the god's (possibly unreliable!) briefing about the world.
- **Sealed tier maps to divine law:** genre's "hero-only feats," class-gated miracles, and "the gods forbid resurrection except by X" are stock. Your component-"laws" gating for sealed feats can be voiced as exactly this.
- **CONFLICT: power-fantasy calibration.** The dominant isekai audience is trained on OP protagonists who never truly lose; Everquill's 2d6 misses, lethal hopeless tier, and permadeath-to-heir sit in the genre's *suffering branch* (Re:Zero, Grimgar of Fantasy and Ash — the canonical "guild fantasy but poverty, fear, and dead party members" reference, worth studying as tonal north star). This branch is prestigious but smaller. Mitigations: market with suffering-branch signals, not OP signals; make the impossible-tier mockery funny (Konosuba license); make the first heir transition happen *early and cheap* so death is taught as a mechanic, not a punishment.
- **CONFLICT: tone lock.** Truck-kun jokes + snarky goddess = Konosuba register; scarred persistent world + heirs = Re:Zero/Grimgar register. Canon supports either, and supports irony-inside-sincerity (narration mocking impossible attempts is a Konosuba move inside a serious world) — but the entry scene sets the contract; write it in the register the whole game keeps.

## 5. Priority summary for Everquill

**Must ship (absence feels wrong):** an explicit summoning/entry scene; a diegetic Status/Codex window in System dress; Appraisal-as-progression; Adventurer's Guild with registration ritual and F–S ranked quest board; a unique entry blessing (make it the Codex itself); reincarnation framing on the heir system.

**Cheap wins (optional, high joy per cost):** `[Skill acquired]`-style toasts for intel unlocks; guild receptionist NPC; blood-drop card scene; rank-plate color progression tied to deed log; god's-waiting-room creation flow; one truck-kun-adjacent wink *only if* the tone is comedic.

**Deliberate deviations to own openly (with canon cover):** no true visible numbers (cover: Appraisal levels + Concealment/False Status canon); no OP combat cheat (cover: Appraisal-is-the-cheat subgenre + Shield Hero/Grimgar hard-mode branch); death matters (cover: Re:Zero/suffering branch); no VRMMO framing (canonically a different genre — correct to avoid).

**Genre-fluency traps to avoid:** true numbers on the player's own sheet only (teaches false trust in numbers); a System that never lies (kills the known/true payoff); rank ladder hard-bound to engine difficulty (rank-mismatch disaster is canon and is your drama); blue-box overuse in narration (documented LitRPG fatigue — keep boxes in Codex, prose in Play).
