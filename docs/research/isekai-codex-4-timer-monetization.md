# Everquill timer/skip economy research report

## Bottom line

World-time gating can work for Everquill, but only if players experience it as **the world continuing while they are away**, not as the game confiscating their ability to play. The strongest precedent is not a conventional stamina bar. It is the combination of idle-game offline progress, life-sim real-time rhythms, and time-saving purchases whose outcome is identical to waiting.

The defensible contract is:

> Slow fictional work takes real time; it always completes safely offline; paying changes only when the result arrives; core conversation and dramatic play remain available; no player is punished for living on a once-daily schedule.

No universal “eight hours is acceptable, nine hours is not” threshold exists. Resentment is driven more reliably by **interruption, schedule coercion, stacked timers, blocked story, and perceived artificiality** than by duration alone.

## Historical evidence: Japan to the global gacha market

### GREE/Mobage established the habit, not blanket acceptance

GREE identifies *Fishing Star* as a 2007 mobile-social-game milestone. Mobage introduced social games in 2009. DeNA’s 2011 annual report describes the model unusually plainly: successful Mobage games provided “ample free-of-charge enjoyment”; paid items saved time; and progression tied to physical time encouraged regular return visits. The intended context was short play during spare moments, not long uninterrupted RPG sessions. [GREE history](https://www.gree.co.jp/jp/en/business/games.html), [DeNA Annual Report 2011](https://dena.com/intl/ir/pdf/other-materials/AnnualReport2011.pdf)

This history explains why stamina became legible to Japanese mobile players, but it does not show that players enjoy arbitrary waits. It shows acceptance when the product promise is:

- brief, repeatable mobile visits;
- generous free participation;
- predictable recovery;
- social/event rhythms;
- payments that substitute money for time rather than deny access outright.

### FGO: tolerated stamina becomes a farming budget

*Fate/Grand Order* regenerates one AP every five minutes and permits recovery with Saint Quartz or several fruit items. A single Saint Quartz has historically been sold for US$0.99, although spending premium summoning currency on AP is generally dominated by freely distributed apples. [Official AP help](https://faq.fate-go.jp/faq/show/1309), [US App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fate-grand-order-english/id1183802626)

The revealing evolution is mitigation:

- frequent campaigns reduce story AP costs, sometimes to zero;
- recovery fruit is regularly distributed;
- the 2022 Bronze Sapling system lets players convert otherwise-wasted AP into a 40-AP fruit for later use.

That turns stamina from “you may not read the story” into “budget or bank your farming.” [Official FGO help/tips](https://news.fate-go.jp/info/tips/), [example zero-AP story campaign](https://news.fate-go.jp/2026/05/2026_new_chapter_cp/)

### Sweep tickets became cheap convenience, not premium drama

In *Princess Connect! Re:Dive*, skip tickets bypass already three-starred battles but still consume stamina. They come from quests, login rewards, mana exchange, and paid packs. Cygames doubled the number awarded through mana purchases in 2019. The Japanese ¥1,000 Daily Jewel Pack currently includes 10 tickets per day for 30 days—300 total—alongside 2,000 jewels and doubled dungeon drops. A ¥480 seven-day stamina pack includes 50 tickets plus stamina. [Cygames ticket increase](https://priconne-redive.jp/news/information/3798/), [official Daily Jewel Pack](https://dmg.priconne-redive.jp/daily/), [official 2022 price schedule](https://priconne-redive.jp/news/information/19610/)

The distinction matters:

- a **sweep ticket** removes repetitive human execution after mastery;
- a **time skip** advances an unresolved world state.

JP precedent says routine QoL should be abundant. Everquill should not make players pay merely to avoid rereading or replaying resolved content.

### Genshin: mobile convention collided with PC/console expectations

At launch, *Genshin Impact* generated 180 Resin per day but capped storage at 120, filling in only 16 hours. That forced efficiency-minded players to log in more than once daily. Coverage explicitly noted that Resin felt more intrusive to PC/console players unfamiliar with mobile stamina. The cap rose first to 160 and then officially to 200; at one Resin per eight minutes, today’s cap represents 26 hours 40 minutes, permitting a once-daily schedule. [Launch-era analysis](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/genshin-impact-is-updating-how-resin-works-but-players-arent-happy/1100-6483839/), [official 200-cap announcement](https://www.hoyolab.com/article/28920205), [current official regeneration rate](https://support.hoyoverse.com/hc/en-us/articles/50333934832537-Effective-Resin-Management)

The relative success came from separating content types: Resin gates reward farming, bosses, domains, and character progression, but not ordinary exploration, story quests, or side missions. Early players barely noticed Resin while substantial story and exploration remained; resentment peaked when Resin-gated farming became most of the remaining endgame. [PC Gamer launch analysis](https://www.pcgamer.com/genshin-impacts-endgame-grind-is-as-frustrating-as-it-is-unnecessary/), [Forbes Resin analysis](https://www.forbes.com/sites/paultassi/2020/10/12/genshin-impact-has-a-resin-problem/)

## Observed resentment thresholds

| Threshold | Evidence | Everquill interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **More than one required check-in per day** | Genshin’s original 16-hour cap wasted generation for once-daily players; later caps moved beyond 24 hours. | Nothing valuable should expire, overflow, or become inefficient because the player checks once daily. |
| **Moment-to-moment play interrupted by long recovery** | *Hogwarts Mystery* was criticized for roughly 90 seconds of tapping followed by about an hour of energy recovery. | Never cut off a conversation, combat, investigation scene, or typed exchange halfway through. |
| **A second timer threatens the progress made while waiting for the first** | *Hogwarts Mystery* attached deadlines to lessons while energy recovered, causing failed tasks and restarts. | Completion timers must have no expiry, failure state, or claim deadline. |
| **Four-to-24-hour waits appear immediately on basic core verbs** | *Dungeon Keeper Mobile* put four- and 24-hour waits on excavating individual tiles; critics contrasted this with seconds in the original. | Hours are defensible for journeys, healing, commissions, or archive research—not opening a door, speaking, or inspecting an object. |
| **All meaningful progress is simultaneously blocked** | The UK ASA concluded that *Dungeon Keeper* frequently left non-spenders unable to take meaningful progressive action until timers finished or were paid away. | One slow project may run, but the character/world must retain other meaningful agency. |
| **Longer waits and higher skip costs escalate together** | The ASA specifically cited increasingly long timers and increasingly expensive skips as progression continued. | Do not make late-game actions slower merely because late-game players are more invested. |
| **The timer is compatible with the fiction but incompatible with the play contract** | *Dragon Age: Inquisition’s* real-time war-table operations were diegetic and completed offline, yet no-wait mods became popular among players treating it as a bingeable premium RPG. | Diegesis cannot excuse blocking the kind of play Everquill advertises. |

## Why buying time can read fairer than buying power

A time purchase has a comprehensible counterfactual: “I could wait and obtain exactly this.” That preserves the patient player’s entitlement and lets the buyer frame the purchase as valuing their own schedule.

AFK’s strongest framing is especially clean: a Fast Reward grants the same 120 minutes of resources that absence would have generated. The transaction changes the clock, not the reward table. Fallout Shelter similarly sells acceleration of travel, return, crafting, and training through an established in-world consumable.

This fairness disappears if earlier access compounds:

- accelerated players complete more production cycles;
- early completion improves event ranking or secures scarce rewards;
- more timers completed means more permanent stats;
- social or PvP comparison makes calendar progress competitive.

Research on competitive-game discussions finds that players focus on whether purchases materially disturb balance and whether free time/effort can reach the same result. Even time savings can be classified as pay-to-win when the accumulated lead becomes practically unrecoverable. [CHI PLAY fairness study](https://yvettewohn.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/2022_chiplay.pdf)

A theoretical analysis of skip pricing also argues that very cheap skipping can lower the perceived value of work and rewards: high player utility, high revenue, and low purchase rates can coexist when most patient players wait and only a small impatient segment buys. This supports skips being useful but non-routine, not the default way to play. [“Pay to (Not) Play”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10205)

## Diegetic framing: what landed and what did not

### Stronger framings

- **Fallout Shelter:** travel takes travel time; training takes training time; crafting takes labor; Nuka-Cola Quantum is an existing fictional product with an intuitive “impossible energy drink” function. Progress continues offline.
- **Animal Crossing’s main series:** the real-world clock is part of the life-sim fantasy, while fishing, collecting, decorating, and socializing remain available. Waiting produces anticipation rather than total inactivity.
- **The Simpsons: Tapped Out:** characters perform humorous, character-specific jobs while absent. The timer represents visible behavior in Springfield, not an abstract depleted bar.
- **AFK Arena:** the entire product promise is that rewards accumulate while the “busy” player is away. Waiting is not disguised deprivation; it is the advertised genre.

### Framings that were insufficient

- **Pocket Camp:** furniture crafting is fictionally justified, but a systems review found that the experience degraded as craft times lengthened and described the attached purchase prompt as unfriendly. [GameDeveloper systems review](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/systems-review---animal-crossing-pocket-camp)
- **Dragon Age’s war table:** sending advisors across a continent plausibly takes hours, but players still resented real-time waits in a premium, bingeable single-player RPG.
- **Hogwarts Mystery:** “attending class” is diegetic, but energy vanishing mid-peril or mid-lesson exposes the transaction more strongly than the fiction.
- **Dungeon Keeper:** excavation is a plausible slow verb, but applying 24 hours to an individual tile contradicted the franchise’s established rhythm.

The operative rule is therefore: **diegesis multiplies a fair timer; it does not launder an unfair one.**

## Eleven evidence-backed rules for Everquill

1. **Gate slow projects, never the conversational heartbeat.** Travel, convalescence, smithing, translation, surveillance, and deep archive research qualify. Dialogue, tactical choices, combat turns, examining nearby objects, and resolving the immediate consequence of a typed action do not. Genshin’s separation of story/exploration from Resin farming is the safer precedent.

2. **Put timers after a payoff, not before one.** A session should end after the player chooses a destination, commissions the blade, or forms the research question—not after a cliffhanger followed by “come back tomorrow.” Hogwarts Mystery demonstrates how interruption makes the purchase feel like ransom.

3. **Preview the duration before committing the action.** Free-form typing creates ambiguity. Offer a fictionally worded choice such as “A cursory search can be done tonight; a complete concordance will take eight hours.” Never reinterpret an innocent verb into an unseen 24-hour obligation.

4. **Design around one daily visit.** Use lifestyle-aligned bands—roughly 2h, 8h, 20–24h, and 48–72h—rather than awkward intervals engineered for check-ins. Nothing should decay, overflow, become less efficient, or expire if the player returns once per day. Genshin’s move from a 16-hour to a 26h40 storage window is the clearest schedule-coercion lesson.

5. **Completion must be automatic, offline, permanent, and safe.** Generate and store the result when the timer ends; send one optional push; show it next time the player opens. Do not require a claim tap, restart the timer, impose a second deadline, or punish a missed notification.

6. **A long project must not freeze the whole fantasy.** Permit other characters, local conversations, journal review, equipment planning, or a limited number of independent world projects. Dungeon Keeper’s regulatory failure was not simply “24 hours”; it was reaching states with no meaningful progressive action.

7. **Show fictional progress, not only digits.** An archive search can move through “cataloguing → cross-referencing → translation”; a caravan can visibly cross named regions. The timer detail may show an exact ETA, but the main presentation should describe what the world is doing.

8. **Paid time must never create better outcomes—or compounding competitive power.** Same model route, context, odds, loot table, prose depth, and consequences whether waited or skipped. Avoid ranked progression, scarce first-arrival rewards, timer-dependent event advantages, and economies where skips enable extra permanent production cycles.

9. **Sell one transparent conversion.** Display “removes eight hours; US$ equivalent X” at purchase. Avoid currency-within-currency conversions and individualized prices. The FTC’s Genshin complaint specifically highlighted how multiple exchanges and unusual denominations obscured real cost. [FTC complaint](https://search.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/cognosphere_complaint.pdf)

10. **Make occasional free skips part of the trust contract.** Give one meaningful skip when the system is introduced and a modest earnable supply thereafter. AFK’s first daily Fast Reward, FGO’s apples, and Princess Connect’s abundant tickets all let players understand convenience before spending. Do not attach the free supply to mandatory twice-daily login behavior.

11. **Consume the item only when a valid result is durably delivered.** If generation fails, refuses incorrectly, loses context, or produces a corrupted result, recredit automatically. A player who paid to accelerate a poor LLM response will perceive the purchase itself—not merely the generation—as defective.

## Provisional Everquill price anchors

Use a fixed, public, declining per-hour curve based on **remaining time**, with the last 10–15 minutes completing free. Avoid Genshin-style same-day price escalation and Fallout’s “2h01m costs twice as much” cliff.

| Remaining world time | Provisional retail equivalent | Rationale |
|---:|---:|---|
| Up to 2h | **$0.49–$0.69** | Between Fallout’s very low rate and Pocket Camp’s short-skip rate; suitable for convenience, not constant use. |
| Up to 8h | **$1.29–$1.79** | Near Genshin’s first eight-hour-equivalent refresh; a plausible “continue tonight” purchase. |
| Up to 24h | **$2.49–$3.49** | Between Fallout/Clash and Pocket Camp’s higher 24-hour anchor. |
| Up to 72h | **$4.99–$5.99** | Strong long-duration discount, close to historic Clash’s week-skip anchor and well below Pocket Camp’s expensive 72-hour craft. |

A diegetic item can encode these bands—for example, one measure for 2h, three for 8h, six for 24h, ten for 72h—but the UI should always expose the cash-equivalent cost. Suggested pack architecture:

- $1.99 entry pack: approximately four 2-hour measures;
- $4.99 core pack: approximately twelve;
- $9.99 value pack: approximately thirty;
- no $49.99/$99.99 “whale truck” at launch;
- no limited-time discount presented while the player is blocked.

### Mandatory compute-cost floor

Let:

- \(K\) = p95 expected incremental LLM/serving cost triggered by the skip, including the immediate completion and reasonably attributable follow-on actions;
- \(s\) = store/payment fee rate;
- \(m\) = required contribution margin.

Then require:

\[
P_{\text{list}} \ge \frac{K}{(1-s)(1-m)}
\]

At a 30% platform fee and a 60% contribution-margin target, the list price must be at least **3.57× \(K\)**. If that floor exceeds the market band above, do not sell the skip at an implausible price: reduce inference cost, batch or route the action differently, shorten the generated result responsibly, or leave that timer non-skippable with clear advance disclosure.

## Western and anime-adjacent audience differences

The meaningful split is expectation, not nationality:

- JP mobile-native players have two decades of literacy around AP, event calendars, recovery fruit, and sweep tickets.
- Western city-builder and idle audiences accept timers when the game openly promises short visits and offline production.
- Western PC/console-adjacent players compare an RPG with full-price or premium narrative games and expect to play on demand.
- Anime/gacha-adjacent western players understand stamina exceptionally well, but that also makes them quick to identify disguised stamina, calculate dollar-per-hour rates, and label compounding acceleration pay-to-win.
- Everquill’s typed-action interface increases the expectation of responsiveness. Players may compare it to chat before they compare it to FGO.

Genshin is the strongest warning: one system can feel routine to mobile-gacha veterans and alien to players attracted by open-world RPG presentation. Dungeon Keeper’s backlash was intensified because an established PC franchise had changed a seconds-long core verb into a 24-hour mobile timer; its reported Metacritic user score fell to roughly 0.2/10 during the controversy, and the UK ASA ruled its “free” advertising misleading in light of timer frequency, length, and monetization. [Contemporary score report](https://www.tomshw.it/smartphone/parla-il-padre-di-dungeon-keeper-il-remake-mobile-e-ridicolo), [ASA coverage](https://www.engadget.com/2014-07-02-uk-regulator-rules-free-dungeon-keeper-ad-was-misleading.html/)

## Top three backfire modes

### 1. “The first session was bait; now the chatbot charges me to answer”

A generous timer-free opening followed by an abrupt wall can look like deliberate conversion engineering. Typed action makes the artificiality unusually visible: the player knows an LLM could respond immediately and may interpret every fictional delay as a withheld API response.

**Likely symptoms:** reviews comparing Everquill to Hogwarts Mystery or Dungeon Keeper; accusations of “paying not to play”; first-timer churn; players testing shorter phrasings to evade classification.

**Mitigation:** introduce world-time through an optional, rewarding slow project before it blocks anything; preview duration; provide the first skip; preserve immediate local play; never place the first timer on a cliffhanger.

### 2. “Time skip” becomes pay-to-power through compounding

Even with unchanged odds, earlier results allow more actions, more crafting cycles, earlier event access, or faster permanent progression. Anime/gacha communities will model this quickly. If a paying player can live three fictional weeks while a non-payer lives one, “never improves odds” will sound technically true but substantively false.

**Likely symptoms:** whale/F2P progression charts, pressure to buy every efficient skip, spend-shaming, demands for leaderboards to exclude accelerated accounts.

**Mitigation:** keep the consequential game primarily solo; eliminate scarce-timing rewards; limit permanent output to the same world-calendar budget where necessary; audit every skip for second-order acceleration, not merely its immediate reward.

### 3. Players pay to accelerate an expensive but disappointing LLM outcome

A skip may unlock a response that misunderstands intent, repeats itself, refuses incorrectly, contradicts memory, or immediately starts another timer. That converts ordinary model disappointment into a monetary grievance. Meanwhile, pricing low enough to feel harmless may fail to cover compute-heavy follow-on chains; pricing high enough to protect margin may expose how little content the player receives per dollar.

**Likely symptoms:** refund requests, “I paid $2 for three paragraphs” screenshots, prompt-engineering to maximize value, skip→timer chains described as double-dipping, or unit losses from highly active buyers.

**Mitigation:** charge only on successful durable delivery; automatically recredit failures; prohibit an immediate second paid wall in the same narrative beat; meter unit economics using p95 downstream compute; and refuse to launch a skip band whose cost floor exceeds a credible market price.
