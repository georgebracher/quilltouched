# 11 — Travel Network

Scope: the map in carriage-days — routes, chokepoints, and travel times as world-clock costs; distance drives rumor distortion, library scope, and timer bands (roadmap §§3.4, 3.6, 3.10). **v1 (B1, Sprint 1): Harrowdown's connections and rhythm. Dunmere, Cranholt, and Severwick are named stubs — do not author their interiors until their sprint.**

## The vale's position

Harrowdown sits where the River Lund cuts through the chalk downs. Three roads leave Market Row (`loc.harrowdown.market_row`); everything the vale knows about the world arrives on one of them.

## Routes

| Road | To | Carriage-days | Chokepoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Cairn Road (`loc.harrowdown.cairn_road`) | Dunmere (`loc.dunmere.market_square`) | 2 | Sedge Ford |
| The South Road (`loc.harrowdown.south_road`) | Severwick (`loc.severwick.market_square`) | 4 | The Lund bridge at Stanwick |
| The Drove Road (`loc.harrowdown.drove_road`) | Cranholt (`loc.cranholt.market_square`) | 3 | High downs, weather |
| The Quarry Track (`loc.harrowdown.quarry_track`) | Dead end at the Redcliff quarry | — | The Quarryman (`creature.harrowdown.quarryman`) |

**The Cairn Road** runs west under Hollow Tor and its barrow field. Carters pass the tor by daylight and do not overnight below it; a laden cart that misses the morning start at either end waits a day rather than camp there. **Sedge Ford** is the crossing: 5 pennies a cart to the Dunmere-side warden, impassable for days after storm-rain. The ford has drowned enough people to have a name in three parishes — Reyla Ferrier among them, thirty years back (`prop.harrowdown.reyla_grave`).

**The South Road** follows the Lund downstream: coaching stops at Stanwick and Ford Bridge, two inns, and the only stone bridge in the vale's reach. This is the artery — the weekly market wagon, the guild wagon before Quarterdays, the yearly roll-copy barge running beside it on the river.

**The Drove Road** climbs over the downs. No carriage service; the 3-day figure is a carrier cart's pace in fair weather. Sheep drives at the season turns, Cranholt timber sledges in autumn, and the Blotted camp somewhere along it most of the year (`05-skill-church.md`).

**The Quarry Track** branches off the Drove Road below Redcliff Scar and has been a dead end for nine years. Grass grows in the wheel ruts. The board's warrant (`prop.harrowdown.guild_board`) is the only traffic it generates.

## Rhythm

- **Weekly:** the Severwick market wagon, out on market-day morning, back six days later. Carriage seats at 30 pennies a day book through Maren at the Drowned Ram, who takes a penny for the booking and all the news the passenger heard.
- **Every eighth day:** the Dunmere carrier cart, weather and ford permitting.
- **Quarterly:** the guild wagon to the Severwick trials, leaving seven days before each Quarterday. The drives move at the same season-turns, which doubles traffic and tavern takings.
- **Yearly:** the roll-copy barge to the Severwick cathedral (`05-skill-church.md`).

## Rumor moves at carriage speed

News is cargo with no freight charge. A deed in Harrowdown reaches Dunmere in two days as fact, Severwick in four as a story, and the capital in a season as a ballad with the names wrong. Distance is the distortion engine (roadmap §3.4); a careful listener prices news by how many carriage-days it took to arrive.

The same gradient governs research: the vale's deepest archive is one roll cabinet and whatever the guild hall keeps in its ledger drawer. Severwick holds the cathedral rolls and the monastery record-halls — including the kingdom's wyvern-hunt archives, the standing lead for anyone serious about the Quarryman (`09-bestiary.md`). Peak knowledge lives near where the thing happened or where the records were carried. Four carriage-days is the real price of a good answer.

## Neighbor stubs

Named, connected, unauthored. One line each, binding until their own sprint:

- **Dunmere** — fen-edge wool town, two days west. Chronic rat trouble; the guild pays standing bounties, third season running. (The Ratcatcher title in the System diction sheet was minted there.)
- **Cranholt** — timber holt over the downs, three days northeast. Sledges in autumn, snowed off part of winter.
- **Severwick** — river port and seat: crown mint, cathedral, guild seat, monastery record-halls. Four days south, and the direction the word "civilization" points when the vale uses it.
