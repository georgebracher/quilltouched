# 05 — Skill Church

Scope: the public appraisal ceremonies run by the Open Page orthodoxy — a ceremony writes a *fallible public KNOWN entry* on you; "the church misjudged me" is the trash-skill fantasy with epistemic teeth (playbook §4 TWIST; doctrine lives in `06-quill-cosmology.md`). **v1 (B1, Sprint 1): the ceremony, the roll entry format, the Harrowdown chapel, and the roll fire.** Later sprints add the Severwick cathedral chapter and inter-parish roll traffic.

## The chapel

The Chapel of the Open Page (`loc.harrowdown.chapel`) is the vale's one church: a stone room, a lectern, the roll cabinet, and a walled yard of graves. Sister Odile (`npc.harrowdown.sister_odile`) has run it alone for nine years. She keeps the rolls, performs the ceremonies, reads the dead their lines, and writes letters for anyone who asks, in that order of priority.

Doctrine in one line: the Quill wrote the world, and record is reverence. What the parish does is write things down, publicly, and stand behind the writing.

## The appraisal ceremony

Held on Quarterday eves. Anyone may present, once a season, for a 10-penny offering. The rite is public by design — an appraisal nobody witnessed is doctrinally worthless.

The order of it:

1. The subject states the name they will be entered under.
2. The sister lays the subject's hand on the blank page of the parish book and reads aloud what surfaces. At Odile's tier, that is the shape of a person: where their weight sits, category-level, no bands, no numbers.
3. A drop of the subject's blood seals the true-name to the entry. This is the sealing that makes the entry an identity record (`02-universe-laws.md`, `law.resurrection`, identity component).
4. The sister writes the entry, signs it, dates it, and reads it back to the room.

The subject does not get to argue with step 4. Corrections require a new ceremony, next season, and the old entry stays on the roll with a strike through it. The Page does not use erasers.

## What a public KNOWN entry looks like

> **Roll of the Harrowdown parish, autumn Quarterday eve.**
> Subject: Bett Harrow, seventeen, carter's daughter.
> Read: weight in the hands and the will. No craft-mark surfaced. Fit for road work and for stubbornness.
> Read by Sister Odile, tier two, her own hand.
> Scope: what the Page showed this parish, this evening. It showed no more.

Every entry carries the reader, the tier, the date, and a scope line. The scope line is the church's honesty about its own limits, and parishioners quote it at each other as proverb: *it showed no more.*

## The entry can be wrong

The rolls are institutional KNOWN, and the church says so out loud. The error mechanisms are causal, never random:

- **Reader tier.** Odile reads at tier two: categories, never bands. A deep talent that surfaces no craft-mark at tier two reads as "no craft-mark," and the entry follows the person around for years. This is the misjudged-by-the-church story, and the vale has a living one — ask at the tavern about the smith's boy the parish read as "fit for lifting" who now signs his letters from a war-college nobody can verify.
- **Transcription.** Entries are copied for the Severwick cathedral once a year, by hand, on a barge.
- **The subject lied about the name.** The blood seals what was said. A false name makes a true record of a false claim, and untangling one is scribe-work measured in seasons.

## The rolls and the fire

The roll cabinet holds thirty years. The winter fire took the roll room and every entry before it: births, appraisals, marriages, true-name sealings, all of it. The cause was never settled, and Odile's predecessor died the same winter without writing down what he suspected. What the fire means in practice: **no person in the vale who died more than thirty years ago has a surviving church identity record.** For the Five Laws of Return, that closes a road (`02-universe-laws.md`). It is also why a water-ruined crate of pre-fire rolls surfacing on a Severwick barge is worth silver to somebody (see the region's rumor seeds).

Reyla Ferrier's grave stands in the chapel yard (`prop.harrowdown.reyla_grave`). She drowned the winter of the fire. Her ceremony record burned with the rest.

## The Blotted in the vale

A Blotted camp moves along the Drove Road with the drovers, a dozen people who sign nothing and are entered on no roll. The parish position is polite and firm: the chapel buries their dead if asked, unrecorded, and does not report the camp to anyone. Odile trades them candle-stubs for wool and does not write it down. This is further than doctrine strictly allows, and she knows it.

## The Quill-touched

Doctrine holds that one who sees the System bears the prophet's mark: read *to*, where everyone else is only written *about*. Odile has never met one, and has thought carefully about what she would do. Her honest answer: enter the fact on the roll, because record is reverence — and sit with what it means that some bishops would rather she didn't.
