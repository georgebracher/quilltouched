# 08 — Demon Lord Dossier

Scope: the flagship pinboard hypothesis — real, dead, committee, or propaganda; the church's answer and the truth are authored separately and this file holds both (playbook §4 TWIST). **Filled to universe depth (Universe Canon v1, 2026-07-14) — this file is no longer a stub.** The KNOWN half is narrator-safe; the **TRUE half never reaches narrator context**. Research domain: `content/canon/texts-knowledge.yaml`, `knowledge.demon_lore`. Canon text: `text.demon_lord_dossier`.

## Why this is the flagship

"Is the Demon Lord real, dead, a committee, or church propaganda?" is the world-class hypothesis-pinboard question (playbook world-furniture §5). It is the best expression of the TRUE/KNOWN split at kingdom scale: the church's answer and the truth can differ, and the game holds both without confirming either. It ties the summoning history (`07`), the calamity-class top of the ladder (`content/canon/bestiary.yaml`, `bestiary.dragon_ancient`), and the bottomless Quill mystery (`06`) into one pinboard.

## The four hypotheses (the pinboard shape)

Every faction's dossier is somewhere on this board. The board itself is narrator-safe; which corner is TRUE is not.

- **Real** — a singular being, alive, that the Calling was raised against and did not finish.
- **Dead** — real once, ended by the summoned heroes, and kept alive as a threat by those who profit from the fear.
- **Committee** — never one being; a succession or a coalition wearing one legend, so the threat never dies because there was never a single thing to kill.
- **Propaganda** — a fabrication, or a real small thing inflated to kingdom scale, to justify levies, summonings, and the crown's grip.

## The church's answer (KNOWN — narrator-safe)

The Open Page's official dossier, preached and archived, confident and internally coherent — and, like every KNOWN sheet, capable of being wrong:

- **The Demon Lord is real and it endures.** The Calling was raised against it; the heroes bought the kingdom time, not victory. Vigilance is doctrine: the levies, the warrants, the seat's standing armies are all justified by its continued existence.
- **It commands the mazoku** (`content/canon/peoples-factions.yaml`, `people.mazoku`) from a territory beyond the frontier the vale has never seen.
- **The prophet's mark matters here.** Some bishops hold that the Quill-touched can read what is coming; some fear that a Quill-touched who looked too hard at the Demon Lord question would read something the church would rather keep sealed.
- **The contradictions a careful reader finds** (narrator-safe, because they are in the record itself): the dossier's descriptions of the Demon Lord do not match across sources or across generations; the "same" being is recorded with different powers, different territories, different deaths. The church explains this as the enemy's deceptions. A careful reader files that explanation and keeps reading (`00-spine.md`: institutions are fallible KNOWN-sheet builders).

The narrator may surface any of the above with provenance ("per the church's dossier"). The narrator may NOT surface which hypothesis is true.

## The truth (TRUE — engine and authoring only, NEVER narrator context)

**Resolved (George, 2026-07-14, `content/canon/decisions-resolved.md` decision 1): the Demon Lord is a dead myth and/or propaganda. It is not a living being.** It is real-once-and-now-dead, or it was never real at all — and either way the legend is kept walking on purpose. The question the world never closes is not whether it lives (it does not), but which kind of not-alive it is, who keeps the story going, and why they cannot let it stop.

Two readings sit under the KNOWN board, and the canon does not have to choose between them cleanly. Both end in the same place: a threat that is gone and a legend that outlived it.

- **Dead.** There was a real thing once. The Calling was raised against it and the summoned heroes ended it, at the price the ballads still flinch from (`07`). It has been gone for generations. The church that preaches its endurance is not lying so much as trusting a dossier it inherited and never checked against a body it never saw.
- **Never-real.** There was no single lord. A smaller real thing — a bad decade of raids, a calamity-class beast (`content/canon/bestiary.yaml`, `bestiary.dragon_ancient`), a border war — got written up into a kingdom-ending enemy, because a kingdom-ending enemy is useful. The contradictions a careful reader finds are the seams where the story was stitched, which the church reads as the enemy's own deceptions.

**The maintained legend is the constant.** Dead or never-born, someone keeps it upright. The levies, the warrants, the standing armies, the crown's grip, the churches' authority, and the summonings themselves all draw their justification from an enemy that is not out there. That is the hook, and it is *cui bono*, not a monster hunt: the deepest tier of `knowledge.demon_lore` points at who profits from the fear and why the profit never lets the story end.

**Why it stays bottomless (`06` hard rule):**

- There is no body to find and no victory to win, so the domain can never bottom out on a kill. It bottoms toward the maintenance instead. Find the dead thing's grave, or the fabrication's first draft, and the next question is who has fed the legend since; answer that and the next is who taught them; and that road runs back toward the crown, the war-colleges, and the Calling.
- It edges the summoning wound (`07`) and the Quill (`06`) without resolving either. Ask hard enough why the enemy must never die and you are asking why the crossings never stopped and who is served by a world kept at war — a road that points at the Quill's nature and is never allowed to arrive (`06`).

**Who benefits (the researcher's pinboard — engine/authoring only):**

- the crown — levies, warrants, and the annals it controls; implicated hardest by the *dead-and-farmed* reading.
- the churches — an enduring enemy is an enduring reason to be obeyed; implicated hardest by the *never-real* reading.
- the war-colleges and whatever still calls souls across (`07`) — a war needs heroes, and heroes need something to be summoned against.

**Still open (texture, not DNA — the DNA is set):** the *mix* and the chief beneficiary are cheap to set later. Lean the vale's first thread toward *dead-and-farmed* (points at the crown) or *never-real* (points at the church), or keep both live and let research pick the thread. Binding and not waivable: the true half never reaches narrator context, and the road may touch the Quill question but never answers it (`06`).

## Binding notes

- **Two files, one board.** The church answer and the truth are authored separately (above) so a leak of one is not a leak of the other. The engine may hand the narrator the KNOWN dossier; it may never hand over the TRUE half.
- **Research, not revelation.** The `knowledge.demon_lore` tiers move a player from "the threat as preached" through "the contradictions in the dossier" to "a real revelation" — and the deepest tier is bottomless by construction. Deep inquiry accrues forbidden-knowledge exposure and attracts both churches (and worse).
- **No cartoon evil** (`06`, world-furniture §5). Whatever the truth, the church is sincere and fallible, not a villain; corruption is local. The Demon Lord is a genuine question, not a strawman to knock down.
